Tell us about the job.

The operating model

One custom scope. Many dependent handoffs.

What they deliver

Custom casework, reception desks, wall and ceiling panels, wood trim, doors, countertops, solid surfaces, specialty finishes and integrated metal, glass or stone.

Who hires them

Commercial general contractors and construction managers, often working for corporate, healthcare, education, hospitality, government and multifamily owners or developers.

Who they coordinate with

Architects, interior designers, GC project teams, material suppliers, finishers, installers and adjacent trades whose work affects dimensions, sequencing and access.

Where earned profit leaks

Small coordination gaps become expensive custom-work problems.

01

Scope changes outrun the paperwork

Architect revisions, field dimensions, finish selections and coordination decisions alter the work before pricing, notice and authorization catch up.

02

Approval delay consumes production time

Shop drawings, samples, mockups and RFIs remain unresolved while procurement dates and installation commitments stay fixed.

03

Shop and field information separate

Fabrication may proceed from one version while installers, the GC and adjacent trades are working from another set of conditions.

04

Earned work waits to become cash

Stored materials, percent complete, approved changes, punch work and closeout support can lag behind the value already delivered.
